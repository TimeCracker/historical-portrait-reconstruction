# Source policy

## Evidence

Prefer records that identify a specific work, collection, accession, date and
attribution. Institutional custody supports traceability; it does not by itself
prove that facial details match a living sitter.

Record separately:

- The depicted person's identity and lifetime.
- The work's date or date range and basis for that date.
- Artist attribution and qualifications such as "attributed to" or "after".
- Copy lineage, restoration, later inscriptions and digital alterations.
- The record's identification of the sitter and any competing identification.
- Whether the work was contemporary, copied later, narrative, ceremonial or
  invented retrospectively.
- Image resolution, face crop, pose, damage and unreadable details.

Keep unknown values null. Do not upgrade late attribution to contemporary
testimony. Multiple reproductions of one painting remain one evidence chain.
Do not use religious sculpture as a portrait merely because of a later legend.

Inspect the actual pixels, not only search thumbnails or text. Keep the
original download and its hash; retain source and download URLs without
credentials or expiring authorization parameters in public records.

## Historical adjustments

Begin with observable features in the selected painting. Use a primary text
only when its wording is verified and relevant to visible appearance.
Record the text, edition or stable URL, and the adjustment it supports.
Do not turn literary praise, character judgments or physiognomy into
measurements. Do not silently overwrite a clear painted feature to fit a
popular modern depiction.

A late source may be suitable for painting-to-photo translation while weak
evidence of the person's actual appearance. State both facts.

## Rights and attribution

Track the actual image file and its distribution source. A work's age, a
museum's name, or a similar image's license is insufficient to assign this
download a license. Preserve the source page's qualified rights statement.

For public examples, record title, author if known, institution, accession,
source link, rights statement, license link, rights-source link, retrieval
date, file hash, and any crop or other alteration. Do not relicense third-party
images as MIT. Record generated outputs and fonts separately.

When reuse terms are unverified, publish source metadata and a reproducible
workflow without redistributing the image. Add a binary only after checking
its applicable terms. Do not claim museum endorsement.
